A forecaster that has to earn its numbers.

An open-source, agentic system that forecasts natural-disaster risk from public data, validates its own probabilities against history, and iterates until they can be trusted — then turns them into emergency plans.

The forecast unit

Small enough to audit, every part of it a contract term.

P(at least one damaging event of hazard H in region R during period T)

"Damaging" is fixed per contract — by default, property damage of $10,000 or more, or any injury or death, per NOAA Storm Events. Regions are US counties; the period is a month, a quarter or a year. For one state, twenty training years and a quarterly period, that is a few thousand labelled region-quarters: dense enough to calibrate against, small enough to reason about.

The whole loop is contract-driven. The hazard, the geography, the forecast period, what counts as a damaging event, the locked splits and the acceptance thresholds are all declared in a registered contract, and one harness runs against any of them. Phase 0 — harness before model — is complete: the eval plane is built, and its exit criteria are met on real NOAA data for three contracts that differ in every dimension a contract controls.

Region-period probabilities are decision support for readiness. They must never be phrased as predictions of specific events, and nothing here is for deciding whether to act on a hazard that is imminent — that is the National Weather Service and IPAWS.

Why so small
A forecast you can check against thirty years of a public record is a forecast you can be wrong about in public. The eval plane exists to make that cheap.

What a run does

Gather context, take action, verify work, repeat — against a contract.

contractsha256 of the criteriahazard · scope · period · damage · splits · thresholds, as JSON
data planefetch · checksum · pinStorm Events + Census + NWS zones
labelled panelregion × period → 0/1dense: the Census supplies the zeros
modelsproposetraining years only, through TrainingView
harnessscore · screen · judgeBrier, BSS, reliability, AUC, canary, verdict
ledgerhash-chainedone per contract, anchored, committed

gather context

Read the contract and its hash. Build the labelled panel from pinned data and report where every event went. Read the ledger of what has already been tried.

take action

Propose a candidate model from the engine's registry. It is fitted through a view of the training years only and asked to predict bare units.

verify work

Labels are fetched only after predict() returns. The scorecard is judged against the contract's clauses and screened by the leakage canary.

repeat

Every experiment is a sealed card in the contract's ledger — including the failures. The loop stops when a candidate passes on validate, or when the queue is exhausted.

make loop CONTRACT=tornado-ok runs this end to end with no language model in the control flow. The same loop runs with a Claude Agent SDK orchestrator and three read-only subagents; the harness is identical either way.

Read the tables as assertions about the harness, not as forecasts

  1. The reference scores exactly 0.0000 / 0.5000 on every contract, and still fails every contract — because being climatology is not beating it.
  2. The same seasonal model lands differently on each hazard. On the flood contract it has slight skill and misses the AUC floor; on tornadoes it discriminates well and fails only on calibration; on monthly tropical cyclones it passes, because the season is so sharp that region plus month clears every clause. A passing contract is permission to spend one test touch, not a claim of a forecast.
  3. The persistence baseline is rejected on every contract, for a different clause each time.
  4. The leaky oracle is rejected everywhere, tripping all four canary checks. Running the canary across base rates from 8% down to 0.66% is what exposed — and fixed — a check that had been calibrated to one hazard.

Why there is no model yet

Harness before model.

Phase 0 — Build the eval plane first, with no forecasting at all. Exit when the agent reproduces the climatology baseline's scores bit-for-bit from a clean clone, and the harness rejects a deliberately leaked model (a canary test).

"Iterate until the accuracy is acceptable" is only science if the thing measuring accuracy never moves. Building the scorer first — and proving it rejects a cheater before any real model exists — is what stops the loop from becoming an elaborate way to memorise the past. make verify checks both criteria for a contract:

The blessed fingerprints live in harness_expected/, one file per contract, and record every scorecard number the two climatology baselines produce on the validate split, plus the data version and contract digest they were produced under. If any of it moves, the criterion fails — which is the point. The sandbox recomputes them in your browser and compares.

Two invariants
No model ever receives a holdout label: TrainingView is the only channel a model gets data through. And nothing in the harness calls a language model: verification is rules-based or it is worth nothing. Most of the design follows from these two.

Phase 1 is built; its exit needs the real-data run. The harness now owns a feature channel with a temporal firewall (docs/features.md): sources hand over raw series and static tables, the harness computes every cutoff, and a static layer that has seen the holdout years — FEMA's National Risk Index, published through 2023 — is refused as a finding. Four feature models (logistic, logistic+iso, gbm, gbm+iso) run through readiness loop --queue phase1; readiness promote is the one atomic test touch, and readiness backtest publishes the report (docs/backtest.md) beside the ledger. The criterion is met only when a test card passes and is published, which this repository's own CI does with the pinned data.

Phase 2 is built too; its exit needs the same run. Six national contracts are registered as data beside the three examples, and readiness fleet runs them through the Phase 1 queue in turn, one ledger and one touch budget each. readiness exposure snapshot pins FEMA / ORNL USA Structures counts per county — the table has no sub-county field by construction — and readiness exposure spot-check compares ten sampled counties against assessor counts a person collected. readiness issue refits a promoted model and writes a probability per county for a period the data has reached; readiness brief turns those into a county paragraph that readiness.cite validates before it is written (docs/brief.md). Which four hazards pass is not knowable offline; the briefs page shows where each contract stands.

What this is not

This is decision support. It is never a warning channel. Official alerts come from the National Weather Service and IPAWS. Region-period probabilities must never be phrased as predictions of specific events, and no output here should be used to decide whether to act on a hazard that is imminent. The full set of risks the design commits to carrying — overfitting the loop, biased and drifting ground truth, LLM prose in life-safety documents, licensing hygiene, and the harm that publishing address-level risk can do to the people it is meant to help — is in §7 of the research briefing.

Ground truth: NOAA NCEI Storm Events. Region universe: US Census Bureau. Zone crosswalk: NOAA NWS. All US Government works, public domain — see DATA-LICENSES.md.
