# Per-layer data licence manifest

Report §7, *Licensing hygiene*:

> Microsoft footprints and parts of Open Buildings are ODbL (share-alike); other
> layers are CC BY-4.0 or public domain. Keep derived databases' licenses
> compatible from day one — retrofitting attribution stacks after mixing is
> painful. Publish a per-layer license manifest in the repo.

This is that manifest. It covers every layer the project uses **now** and every
layer the roadmap commits to using, so the compatibility question is answered
before the layer is added rather than after.

The machine-readable version — with a sha256 for every file actually pulled — is
`snapshots/manifest.json`, regenerated by `readiness snapshot`.

### The two obligations that will actually bite

**ODbL (Microsoft footprints, Overture, parts of Open Buildings).** ODbL is
share-alike *for databases*. If a derived database that incorporates ODbL data
is published, it must be published under ODbL with attribution. This is
compatible with Apache-2.0 code — the licences govern different things — but it
means the **published risk database** may carry ODbL even though the **code**
does not.

Practical rule: keep exposure-joined outputs in a separate, clearly-labelled
artefact from the hazard-probability outputs, so a consumer who only wants
probabilities is not handed an ODbL obligation they did not ask for. Decide this
before Phase 2, when exposure joins first appear.

**GPL-3.0 (CLIMADA).** GPL is share-alike for *code*, and it is viral across a
linked work. Report §3E says the agent should "drive, extend, and calibrate"
CLIMADA rather than reinvent it — so keep the integration at arm's length
(subprocess or a separate GPL-licensed adapter package), or accept that the
integrating component becomes GPL. Deciding this at Phase 1, when CLIMADA is
first wired in, is much cheaper than deciding it afterwards.

### The gap worth repeating

**Google Open Buildings does not cover the United States.** Its 1.8B detections
span Africa, South and Southeast Asia, Latin America and the Caribbean. It is
the unlock for Phase 4 and useless for Phases 0–3. Planning a US-first roadmap
around it is a common and expensive mistake.

## Attribution block for published outputs

Any published artefact derived from these layers should carry, at minimum:

```
Hazard history: NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information,
  Storm Events Database (public domain).
Geography: US Census Bureau (public domain).
[if zone events expanded] Zone-county crosswalk: NOAA National Weather
  Service (public domain).
[if exposure joined] Building footprints: (c) Microsoft / Overture Maps
  contributors, ODbL 1.0. This derived database is available under ODbL 1.0.
[if risk engine used] Risk computation: CLIMADA, ETH Zurich, GPL-3.0.
Not an official warning product. Official alerts come from the US National
Weather Service and IPAWS.
```

That last line is not a licence obligation. It is in this file because it should
appear everywhere the others do.
